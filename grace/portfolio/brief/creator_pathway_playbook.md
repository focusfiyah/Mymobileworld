What a UGC Portfolio Actually Needs to Do
A UGC portfolio is not a scrapbook and it is not your life story. It is a hiring tool. A brand or agency should be
able to open it and quickly understand what you create, whether your work fits their product, whether you
look professional to work with, and how to contact you.
The five questions your portfolio should answer
 Can this creator make content we could actually use?
 Are they believable for our product and customer?
 What types of creative can they produce?
 Do they look professional and easy to work with?
 What is the next step if we want to hire them?
 The goal is not to make the fanciest website. The goal is to make it easy for someone
 to evaluate your work and take the next step.
Keep the portfolio focused
Your portfolio can be broad. You do not have to force yourself into one niche. But your examples should support the work you
want. If you want food and beverage work, include at least one strong food or beverage example when possible. If you want
skincare work, show that you can make skincare content. Your portfolio should prove the type of work you want to be hired to
create.

Writing Each Section
Hero
Keep the headline short. It should communicate value quickly, not simply announce that you are a UGC creator. The supporting
paragraph should explain what you create, what makes you useful, and what the brand can expect.
Avoid generic language such as “passionate creator,” “authentic and engaging content,” or “bringing your brand's vision to life.”
Specific information is stronger.
Meet the Creator
Do not write a long biography. Focus on why your real life makes you useful to a brand. Examples include professional expertise,
being a parent, access to a pet, fitness experience, cooking, a useful location, or a specific skill.
Categories
Use roughly 4 to 7 categories that are believable for you. Do not list every category you could technically create in. Categories
should help a brand quickly understand your natural fit.
What to Expect
Three steps are usually enough. Keep the process simple: brief, concept/direction, delivery. State your real turnaround time and
revision policy. Do not promise a timeline you cannot consistently meet.
Final CTA
The viewer has already seen your work. Do not write another sales pitch. Give them a clear next action, such as sending a brief,
contacting you, or requesting concepts.

Choosing Your UGC Examples
Strong work beats a full grid. Do not include a weak video just because the
template has an empty slot.
Recommended counts
6 strong videos: use a clean 3 x 2 layout.
8 strong videos: a 4 x 2 layout works well.
More than 8: choose the strongest and most useful collection.
Fewer than 6: use what you have cleanly and build additional examples over time.
Label by creative concept
When possible, labels should tell the viewer what kind of creative they are seeing, not only how it was filmed.
EXAMPLES
 Product Demo Problem / Solution Testimonial Product Review Educational How-To Storytelling
 Lifestyle Unboxing Before & After Comparison Listicle Skit Routine App Demo
 Product Showcase Voiceover Talking Head
Check alignment
If there is a category you specifically want to get hired for, try to include at least one relevant strong example. This is not about
niching down. It is about making sure your portfolio proves you can create the work you want.

Buttons, Hyperlinks, Socials, and Email
What is a hyperlink?
A hyperlink is simply a destination attached to text, a button, an icon, or another element. When someone clicks it, they are taken
to that destination.
How to add a link in Canva
Canva's interface changes over time, but the workflow is generally:
01 Select the text, button, icon, or element you want to make clickable.
02 Find the Link option in the current Canva toolbar/menu. It may appear as a link/chain icon or inside a More menu.
03 Enter or paste the correct destination.
04 Apply/save the link.
05 Preview or publish and click it yourself to confirm it works.
 Changing the words on a button does NOT automatically change the old hyperlink
 underneath it.
Links to verify
 TikTok Instagram Email/contact method
 Primary CTA Secondary CTA Any other social/contact icon
CTA logic
The button text must match the destination. “Book a Call” should lead to booking. “Send Me Your Brief” should lead to a place
where a brand can actually contact you or send the brief. Do not use a CTA that promises an action the destination cannot
complete.

Publishing Your Canva Website
Canva's interface can change, so use the current publishing controls shown in your account. You do not need Canva Pro just to
create a usable portfolio. If a feature is paid, use a free alternative when possible.
General publishing flow
01 Preview the website.
02 Check desktop and mobile.
03 Open Canva's current website publishing controls.
04 Review the publishing settings available to your account.
05 Choose the URL/domain option you want.
06 Publish.
07 Open the LIVE public website.
08 Test every important link and video.
 If a tutorial's menu names do not match what Canva currently shows, follow the current
 interface rather than assuming the old tutorial is correct.

Domains, Updating, and Unpublishing
Free Canva URL
The simplest option is the Canva-hosted website address available to your account. This is enough to get a portfolio live. A
custom domain is optional.
Buying a new domain
If Canva currently offers a domain-purchase option in your publishing flow and you want one, follow the current prompts to
search for a domain, review the price, purchase it intentionally, connect it, and publish. Do not assume a domain is available until
Canva confirms it.
Simple naming structures include firstnameugc.com, firstnamelastname.com, ugcbyfirstname.com, or firstnamecreates.com.
Already own a domain
Check whether your current Canva plan supports connecting an existing domain. If it requires a paid plan, using the Canva-
hosted URL is still a valid option.
Updating later
Your portfolio is not permanent. You can continue improving your copy, replacing videos, and updating proof. After meaningful
changes, update/republish using Canva's current controls and test the live site again.
Unpublishing
If you need to take the site offline, use Canva's current unpublish controls. Because interfaces change, use the options visible in
your account rather than relying on an old screenshot or tutorial.
