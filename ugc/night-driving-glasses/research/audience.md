# Audience: night driving glasses (Grace), 2026-10-12

Data: research/audience/questions.md (1,920 autocomplete phrases, Google/Bing/YouTube/Amazon/TikTok), comments.md (219 TikTok
comments from the 5 winners in research/coach/), voc.md (Mumsnet, Daily Dot, SlashGear, CTV quotes).

## Who
Women who drive home after dark, many with glasses or astigmatism, plus the people who buy for them (moms, partners). Top search
questions are doubt questions: "do night driving glasses work", "are night driving glasses worth it", "are night driving glasses a scam".
Commenters ask "Would those fit over glasses?" and "can you wear your prescription glasses with them?"

## Pain point experience
The moment an oncoming car's LED lights hit: "Bright white headlights coming towards me on the road after dark cause a whiteout",
"I always glance down to the left hand road line as cars pass right by." Some quit night driving: "I try to avoid driving after dark now",
"I dont drive at night anymore". Glasses wearers have it worse: "I find the so called anti-glare coating on my glasses makes it much worse."
Anger at the lights: "I call the led headlights terrorist headlights." Many think it is only them: "Is that not how everyone sees?"

## Communication style
Short, relatable, a bit of humor, friend to friend ("This is so real"). They are skeptical of big promises: "I have em. Literally don’t help at all."
and an optician's top comment says yellow glasses won't fix astigmatism. Winners talk in first person about the moment, then show the pair going over regular glasses.

## What it means for the script
Open on the recognizable whiteout moment (H062 filled with "everything goes white"), name the road-line stare and the glasses-wearer pain,
answer "does it fit over glasses" on screen right away, keep the claim soft and exact ("cuts the glare", listing wording), no astigmatism or
"see better" promise, show the side windows as the detail that answers "does it work". Gift close for mom (buyers shopping for others).
