# Audience: Bite Toothpaste Bits (2026-11-03)
Data: audience/questions.md (2,628 autocomplete phrases, Google/Bing/YouTube/Amazon/TikTok), audience/comments.md (207 TikTok comments from the 3 category winners), audience/voc.md (review quotes with URLs).

## Who
Women 25-45 who already buy clean swaps and travel a few times a year: they search "toothpaste tablets for travel", "is bite toothpaste legit", "are toothpaste tablets worth it" and "nano hydroxyapatite vs fluoride". A second group is plastic-guilt buyers: "I'm beyond psyched to have unnecessary plastics removed from my daily routine."

## Pain point experience
Not dramatic, daily: the tube (crusty cap, the blob in the sink, the last squeeze), the TSA bag, plastic guilt. Under it sits doubt about the new format: "do toothpaste tablets work", "Do these ACTUALLY work??? I need ASAP!", "how do you literally chew?", and the funny confusion "You spit it out then?! I've been eating them!". The known letdown is foam: "Isn't as foamy as standard toothpaste" and "Bit hard to get used to the format".

## Communication style
Short, joking, skeptical TikTok talk ("Is this real yall?", "People got a sink right? Not a cup"). They trust a quick demo over claims: chew, brush, foam. They mock staged details (spitting in a cup gets 256 likes of jokes). Plain words; nobody says "remineralizing", they say "does it work" and "is it safe".

## What it means for the script
1. Open on the tube annoyance (H006) or the format surprise, then show the bit in the hand at once.
2. Answer "how do you use it" in one line: chew it, wet brush, brush like normal (listing image 2).
3. Answer the foam doubt honestly but as a sell: "it foams up once you brush" (review words), never a downside.
4. "Does it work / is it safe": say what's in it (nano-hydroxyapatite, xylitol, no sulfates) and what people say ("just as clean as tubed toothpaste"), no medical claims.
5. Travel is the real urgency (holiday trips) and the multi-unit close (one for home, one for the travel bag).
6. In any shot, spit goes in the sink, never a cup.
