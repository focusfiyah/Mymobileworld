# Audience: BodyJ4You castor oil roll-on (Grace), 2026-10-17

## Who
Grace's own viewers from the 2.8M cradle cap video: mostly moms of babies and toddlers, many Black and brown moms who already grew up with castor oil and hair grease ("I used Jamaican black castor oil when my son had it", "apply a little blue magic or Jamaican black castor oil for moisture after you wash"). They already believe in castor oil; the comment "I think we all need castor oil" says they want it for themselves, not only for the baby.

## Pain point experience
They know castor oil is thick and takes work ("So much work! But worth it!"; Grace: "Castor oil is thick"). They care that it is the real thing and clean ("You have to make sure it’s hexane free as well you don’t want to put that stuff on your baby."). Grace's old link is dead, so the people who asked can't buy what she showed.

## Communication style
Casual, mom-to-mom, short and direct, family remedies passed down ("This has worked in my family for yearsssss"). They argue with expert tone (the top comments correct her combing), so Grace should not lecture; she passes things on and answers the comment.

## What it means for the script
Open as a reply to the comment, say cradle cap once as the setup, then move to her own uses (brows, cuticles, elbows, hair ends). Name the thick, messy part as the pain and the roller as the fix. Say "hexane free" and "cold pressed" because this audience checks for it. No baby use, no medical or growth claims (commenters push back on remedies). Close on two places to keep one (purse, nightstand).

## Question map (audience.py, 2,374 phrases, research/audience/questions.md)
Most-agreed questions: "is castor oil good for skin", "which castor oil is best", "is castor oil good for your face", "does castor oil expire", "how to get rid of castor oil smell". People shop on "which one is best", so the clean/cold pressed/hexane free line earns its place; growth, packs and liver questions are claims we stay away from.
