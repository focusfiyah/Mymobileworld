# Audience research (2026-10-05, redone on the new process): Super Blanky wearable blanket
Data: `audience/questions.md` + `audience/raw.json` (1,901 phrases from Google, Bing, YouTube, Amazon and TikTok autocomplete), `audience/comments.md` (259 of 607 TikTok comments reachable, 6 videos), `audience/voc.md` (store reviews; Reddit blocked and Exa rate limited, both tested). Autocomplete shows what they ask. The comments show how they talk.

## Who
- Women, mostly, who run cold at home and at a desk. In the comments: "I need this I'm always cold", and a mom who writes "My mom wore her robe to drive us to school on rainy days."
- Gift buyers for a mom or a parent: "I think I’ll look into this for my elderly mom" and "Perfect Christmas gift".
- Already comparing it to something they know: "So a robe?" Search shows the same: "blanket with sleeves instead of hood" and "wearable blanket hoodie" (5 sources agree).
- Practical buyers who check before buying: "can wearable blankets be washed" and "do wearable blankets keep you warm".

## Pain point experience
1. **The real pain is the blanket that will not stay on while you do things.** "Cozy, snacks, gaming, and no blanket slipping off mid game!" Store buyers say the same thing in other words: "hands-free warmth during activities like gaming or reading".
2. **Doubt before buying is about size, washing and fabric, not about warmth.** The most repeated comments: "How tall r u? This is important", "So im 5’10 is it long enough", "what about washing?? is it safe to wash?" (18 likes, the top buyer question) and "What type of fabric is it?"
3. **They worry it is cheap.** "Is it actually a decent quality and not that cheap Sherpa stuff?"
4. **Wearing it out and about is a question, and a joke.** "Can I wear it at work?" and the mom comments about wearing it to daycare pickup. Humor, no shame.
5. **Heat is not the issue people raise.** Skeptics argue about the weather ("-75? Where are yall living?"), not about the product. Do not add temperature claims.

## Communication style
- Short, plain, friendly, peer to peer. They write "Could it be any cuter!? LOVE", "My literal obsession right now!", "Cool mom for sure."
- They want facts in numbers, then a reassurance: the size, the fabric, can I wash it. Answer these before selling.
- Humor about the look lands well ("It’s a super mom cape"). Grace can be lightly funny, never pushy.
- They answer a hard sell with jokes and doubt ("So a robe?"). Calm demo beats loud claim.
- Gift talk is warm and short: "Perfect Christmas gift".

## What it means for the script
- The product is simple and visual, so no "treatment" WHY-first beat. The structure follows the questions: answer size, washing, fabric and "is it a robe" up front, in numbers, then show it staying on.
- Script 1 is built as the answers to the top buyer questions (size, robe, fabric, wash). Script 2 is the stay-on proof for the always-cold identity. Script 3 is the gift for a mom, comfort framing only.
- Hooks come from the library and match what the comments say: a question before buying (H113), the always-cold identity (H061), thinking of your mom (H056).
- Never say "reviewer(s)". When using what buyers say, say "people say", the way Grace would pass it on.
- Grace must say her own height and where it hits. Do not invent it. That is the number buyers ask for most.
- No temperature promises and no health claims.
