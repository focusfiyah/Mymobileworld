# Audience: Cleanwaste toilet kits (2026-10-21)
Data: audience/questions.md (2,471 autocomplete phrases from Google, Bing, YouTube, Amazon, TikTok), audience/comments.md (133 TikTok comments on the 5 researched Wag Bag videos, incl. Grace's own @shewiththeglow post), audience/voc.md (Amazon review summary, brand listing, preparedness writers).

## Who
Homeowners and parents who already keep some emergency stuff (water, food, flashlights) and think of themselves as ready, plus people on wells or in storm areas. Many have never thought about the bathroom part: "never thought of having these. thank you for sharing" and "Goodness, just another thing to add to my husband‘s doomsday list! But this is something I ever thought of". Search data shows the same blind spot: "can i flush my toilet when the power goes out" and "can i use my toilet during a power outage". The TikTok listing title itself names hurricanes and power outages.

## Pain point experience
The moment is not the outage, it's the first person who needs to go: "Everything, that is, until one of the kids said that she had to go to the bathroom." and "The look of horror and outrage on her face was indescribable." Their fallback is a bucket or a trash bag, which they find gross and undignified: "And nobody looks good perched on a plastic bucket." and "five gallon bucket with garbage bad, and a senior potty chair". They worry about smell ("are wag bags smell proof") and don't know how it works ("so do you put the bag in a bucket?", "You are the first person that described how to use this bag. Thank you so much!").

## Communication style
Funny and blunt wins: the top Wag Bag video (@mamarashaah, 171k) got comments like "lmao 🤣 shes right though, we all need to shit" and "The realist in me loves this! Instant follow!". People like plain talk about poop, but push back on doomsday framing ("Surviving doomsday is overrated") and on anything that sounds like a sales pitch for the apocalypse. Skeptics bring DIY answers ("Or buy kitty litter", "All you need is a shovel and toilet paper") and the flushing objection ("You can still use a toilet and flush by adding water in the back of the toilet you do not need power to flush a toilet."). So: calm, practical, a little funny, no fear.

## What it means for the script
- Open on the blind spot ("did you prepare for the toilet to stop working"): matches "never thought of having these". Keep it calm prep, not doomsday (Grace's own filming tip agrees).
- Answer "how does it work" on screen: waste bag over a regular toilet, powder gels it, zip bag seals it. That is the line people thanked the creator for.
- Don't claim toilets never flush in an outage (commenters correct it). Say "a toilet that won't flush" (brand: "also fits non-working toilets"); V2 names the real cases (no water, a well pump with no power).
- Answer the DIY bucket crowd without knocking anyone: everything is in one kit (bag, powder, paper, wipe, zip bag).
- Count matters to buyers ("How many you think we should buy per person?"): say twelve kits in a box and push a second box (house + car) in V2.
