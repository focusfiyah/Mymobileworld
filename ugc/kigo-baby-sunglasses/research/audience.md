# Audience: KIGO Baby Sunglasses (2026-11-05)
Data: research/audience/questions.md (1405 autocomplete phrases, Google/Bing/YouTube/Amazon/TikTok), voc.md, coach/*.

## Who
Parents of babies and toddlers, mostly moms scrolling TikTok Shop: new moms with a baby shower or first birthday coming, moms of 1 to 4 year olds who drive a lot (car seat), families planning holiday trips (some south, some to snow), and gift buyers (aunts, grandmas, friends) who want a baby gift that is not another outfit. They are asking the basic question first: "are baby sunglasses worth it", "can babies wear sunglasses in the car", "do baby sunglasses need to be polarized".

## Pain point experience
The scene is small and daily: the sun hits the back seat, the child squints and fusses, and the sunglasses they tried either get thrown, bent or pulled off. They search for "baby sunglasses that stay on" and "toddler sunglasses with strap" (their top searches), so staying on and surviving a toddler matter more than looks. The season is the second pain: they ask "can you wear sunglasses in the winter" and "are kids sunglasses winter", so a fall buyer needs a reason beyond summer. A parent blog tells the quiet version: her baby "has always hated sunglasses" so the buyer is also afraid of wasting money.

## Communication style
Short, practical, mom-to-mom. Questions first ("are baby sunglasses safe", "should toddler sunglasses be polarized"), answered in one plain line. No lecture, no medical language. Show it working (bend, twist, put them on). The creator video that mentions the product used the plain words "lightweight, flexible" and "reducing glare during outdoor play".

## What it means for the script
Lead with the exact moment (low sun in the car, a snow day, a gift list) and put them on or bend them in the first 3 seconds. Answer "worth it in winter?" with glare and low sun, not health scares. Say "polarized" and "UV400" once, in plain words. Show survives-a-toddler as proof. Gift and trip urgency are real in November; never fake scarcity.
