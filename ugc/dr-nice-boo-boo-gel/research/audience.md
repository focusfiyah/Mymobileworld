# Audience (2026-11-03): Dr. Nice's Boo-Boo Gel for Kids, 1 fl oz

## Who
Parents of toddlers and young kids (about 2 to 8) who play outside, at the playground, at practice or camp. They keep a first aid stash in the diaper bag or backpack and have tried the usual creams and bandaids. Their searches are plain and practical:
> "what to put on kid bug bite"
> "what can i put on a toddlers bug bite"
> "best bug bite relief for toddlers"
> "boo boo gel for kids"
On TikTok they ask the same thing under the bite videos: "Can toddler use this?"

## Pain point experience
Two pains, and both are about the kid, not the cut. First, the meltdown: the tiny scrape turns into tears, and the bandaid becomes a fight.
> "5+ year olds freak out about every single tiny little scrape."
> "every little injury just sets him off and he cries and cries and cries."
> "she is fighting my husband and I tooth and nail about “looking at her bandaid.”"
The most-viewed kids' first-aid video opens on exactly this: "Your kid melts down every time you clean a scrape." (10.8M views)
Second, the scratching: bites and scabs get picked until they bleed, and nothing stays on.
> "They scratch them until they bleed no matter how much cream I apply."
> "I used an entire box of bandaids between the two of them today"
> "They do peel off 5 minutes later 😂"
> "They slide right off"
The fix parents found on their own is control: "What helped her was letting her do it on her own."

## Communication style
Mom to mom, tired and a little funny about it, short sentences, "my little one", "my kids". They laugh at the chaos and admit the guilt:
> "I did it! 'Nother band aid!"
> "people are going to think I abuse them"
Praise is about calm, not cure:
> "No more tears in our house"
> "It calms my daughter right away"
> "My kids actually ask for it when they get a scrape."
Pushback is about price and cheap swaps, and things that don't stay on:
> "DOLLARTREE HAS THEM FOR 1.25"

## What it means for the script
- Hook on the meltdown, not the product: open on the kid's scrape or bite and the tears, in the parent's words ("melts down every time", "cries and cries and cries"); the "Can toddler use this?" question is answered by the box's own line, "Safe for Kids to Self-Apply".
- The selling moment is the kid doing it themselves: one pump, they dab it on, no fight over a bandaid ("letting her do it on her own"; brand: "No-sting gel kids put on themselves. One pump per boo-boo.").
- Say only the listing's words: "no stinging", "soothes", "cooling", "non-greasy", "four ingredients", "vegan and cruelty-free"; scrapes, bumps, minor cuts first. Bites only as the TikTok Shop title says them ("insect bite"), never "stops scratching", "heals", "scars" or "venom".
- CTA = one for every bag: "Sized for backpacks, diaper bags, and travel pouches" (listing), so a second and third unit make sense. Never a price (the niche's top comments are about price).
