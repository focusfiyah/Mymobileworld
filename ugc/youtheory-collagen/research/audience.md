# Audience: Youtheory Collagen Liquid (2026-10-22)
Data: audience/questions.md (1,826 autocomplete phrases, Google/Bing/YouTube/Amazon/TikTok, seeds "liquid collagen",
"collagen packets"), audience/comments.md (52 TikTok comments on the 4 researched videos), audience/voc.md.

## Who
Busy adults, mostly women 25-45, who already know collagen and are deciding on the format, not on collagen itself.
Search data is full of format comparisons: "liquid collagen vs powder" (4 sources agree), "is liquid collagen better than powder"
, "is liquid collagen better than pills", "are collagen packets worth it". Buyers on the top liquid-collagen
video say the same: "This liquid form is perfect because I’m horrible at taking pills lol" and "It’s better than just
the powder or pills."

## Pain point experience
The pain is the daily chore and the taste of what they use now: powders to mix and pills to swallow. The Youtheory
seller says it plainly: "You don't have to worry about that nasty chalky collagen." Before buying they ask whether it
works and how fast ("When did you start seeing results? Anyone…?", "does liquid collagen work"), whether it tastes good
("Do It Taste Good ?") and how to take it ("how to take liquid collagen"). One commenter repeats a myth our client
script almost fed: "There’s no vitamin C in it so the collagen ain’t even being absorbed".

## Communication style
Girlfriend talk, short and excited, no science lecture: "girl it's time to re-up I'm using your link🥰". They respond to
ease ("All you do is literally shake it, drink it directly.") and to visible proof (the 49.5k video asks "How old do you think I am?"
). Skeptics show up as "I just bought it, I better not be disappointed." so no big promises.

## What it means for the script
- The hook names the powder chore: matches "liquid collagen vs powder" and the pills/powder comments.
- Show the pouch being shaken and drunk straight from the pouch with no glass, no scoop: answers "how to take liquid collagen".
- Say the 5,000 mg number (specific beats adjectives) and "no water needed" (on the box), never vitamin C (not in it).
- No result timeframes and no age claims (the label gives none); "helps reduce fine lines & wrinkles" is the label claim if needed.
- On the go: pouch into a handbag, matches the "packets" searchers and the box line "Simple. Convenient. Portable."
