# Audience research (2026-10-05): Base Laboratories Ingrown Hair Pads
Data: `audience/questions.md` + `audience/raw.json` (2,486 distinct phrases from 744 Google/Bing/YouTube/Amazon autocomplete lookups, AnswerThePublic-style), `audience/voc.md` (verbatim Reddit quotes). Autocomplete shows WHAT they ask; the quotes show HOW they feel and talk.

## Who
- Women who remove hair at home (shave first, some wax, epilate or look at laser) and get bumps on the bikini line, underarms, legs and thighs. Searches: "razor bumps bikini", "ingrown hair underarm", "razor bumps legs", "ingrown hair after waxing", "ingrown hair after brazilian wax".
- Thick or coarse hair and darker skin are a big slice: "razor bumps dark skin", "razor bumps treatment for black women", "ingrown hair hyperpigmentation treatment". Not claimable (no scar or dark-spot claims), but speak to them without excluding them.
- Seasonal buyers: bikini, swimming, dresses, "ruining my summer". Time and money are tight ("I don’t have the time or money to go a professional waxing").
- Already product-aware: they search "ingrown hair pads", "ingrown hair serum", "ingrown hair oil", "ingrown hair glycolic acid", and compare "ingrown hair vs cyst", "razor bumps vs razor burn".

## Pain point experience
1. **Embarrassment and avoidance is the real pain, the bump is only the cause.** "I can’t wear a bikini and I find it so embarrassing." "I’m really insecure about it because I can’t go swimming, I can’t wear nice dresses". They hide, skip swimming, dress around it.
2. **They already do everything right and it still happens.** "I shave in the direction the hair grows, use shaving cream, replace my blade frequently, and exfoliate 2-3 times a week." So "change your razor" advice insults them; a line that says it may not be the razor lands.
3. **They are burned by products that did little.** "I've tried exfoliating and the Fiest Aid Ingrown Hair Pads, but they haven't done much. My hair is so thick!" "all three manage skin but nothing stops the cycle." Skeptical, so no big promises.
4. **Real irritations with other options:** "glycolic acid (The Ordinary) stings but helps the bumps. Tendskin works fast, smells awful." Sting and smell are the competitor pains.
5. **The loop and the season:** "Summer is almost here and my bikini line ingrown hairs problem is back again." "just wish i figured this routine out earlier."
6. **What they ask (top, 3 sources agree):** why do ingrown hairs happen, how to get rid of razor bumps, do they go away, do they hurt, what causes razor bumps, how razor bumps are formed, ingrown hair vs cyst.

7. **TikTok comments on the researched videos (53 of 81 reachable, `audience/comments.md`):** few and short, but the real ones are buyer questions about the experience and the order of use: "Do you save before using? And does it burn?" "so the first you use before the shower?" "How did you open it?" The only proof comment is peer-style: "YESSS these work so good". Takeaways: say what order to use it in (clean, dry skin, after the shower), show the lid opening, and answer "does it burn" only with what the brand says (no non-irritating claim).

## Communication style
- **Explain WHY before selling.** The top questions are "why/what causes/how formed", and the praised answer explains a mechanism: "It works because it has two mechanisms of exfoliation". Plain-word cause first (dead skin and buildup over the hair), then the product doing that job.
- **Peer voice, not expert.** They trade routines like friends ("survival list", "what actually works", "this changed my life"). Grace speaks as the friend passing it on.
- **Name the real ingredients in plain words** (glycolic, salicylic, AHA, BHA): they search them.
- **Empathy for the avoiding, no shame, no before/after, no scar/dark-spot/'cure' claims.** Speak to hiding a bikini line or arms, not to the skin.
- **Honest and low-hype:** they distrust products that "haven't done much". Specific how-to and count beat adjectives. Never promise a timeframe.
- **Season is the real urgency** (bikini, swim, dresses, party), never fake scarcity.
- **Avoid:** "miracle", "game changer", "cure", medical terms (cyst, infection), price, anything that says the razor is the user's fault.

## What it means for the script
- v3 pads A ("Maybe the problem is not your razor", "Change blades, shave slower, switch soap") matches pain #2 almost word for word. Keep.
- Upgrade to consider: they already exfoliate (scrubs, 2-3 times a week), so add one beat on why a scrub is not the same job: AHA works on the dead skin on top and BHA works in the pore (the "two mechanisms" idea, brand-safe wording).
- The "doesn't sting/smell" angle is a real gap in their words, but Base makes no non-irritating claim: do not use unless Grace has used it.
- Thick-hair line for the oil, not the pads ("softens coarse hair").
