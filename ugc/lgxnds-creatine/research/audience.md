# Audience (LGXNDS creatine, 2026-10-18, from grace/audience.py: 3,774 autocomplete phrases + 43 TikTok comments + voc.md)

## Who
Women who are new to creatine or restarting it, many 40+: the strongest seeds are
> "creatine for women over 40"
> "creatine for women over 50"
> "why creatine is good for women"
They are deciding between powder and gummies (> "are creatine gummies better than powder") and want something simple.

## Pain point experience
Worry first, then hassle. They ask
> "does creatine make you bloated"
> "is creatine safe"
and the powder they tried settles at the bottom and tastes like sand (Amazon review in voc.md: "immediately settles to the bottom").
They forget days and ask
> "should creatine be taken on rest days"

## Communication style
Plain, friendly, short. Commenters talk to each other like friends ("don’t use gummy’s. just use the regular powder", 12 likes),
not like lifters. No gym jargon, no loading phases, no science lecture. No "girls" (Ralph 2026-10-18).

## What it means for the script
- Open on texture (the fine powder) because the mix is the one thing a hands-only video can prove on screen.
- Say "five grams in every scoop" and that the scoop is in the tub: removes the "how much do I take" question.
- One line on where it goes (water, shake, coffee: > "creatine in coffee" is a common search).
- Answer the powder-or-gummies question in one short line, and the rest-days question with "every day, rest days too".
- No bloat, kidney or weight claims (not on the label; see product_facts.md).
