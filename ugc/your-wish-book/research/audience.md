# Audience: Your Wish Is Your Command (2026-10-19)
Data: research/audience/questions.md (2,121 autocomplete phrases, `grace/audience.py`), comments.md (238 TikTok comments from 7 book videos), voc.md (listing reviews, creator quotes, web).

## Who
Mostly women who already practice manifestation: they have read The Secret or Think and Grow Rich, made vision boards, journal. Search data: "manifestation book for beginners" (4 sources), "law of attraction book for women" (3), "manifestation book journal" (3). A second group is the gift buyer for that friend. Some are faith-first and wary of the word ("Manifestation? No. FAITH").

## Pain point experience
They know the ideas but get stuck on what to actually do: "what is the actual formula? What am I supposed to do with all of this?" and "just believe harder is exactly where I tend to get". The top search questions are doubt: "do manifestation books actually work", "are manifestation books worth it". In the comments they ask for the how: "When you write down what you want how exactly do you write it? Like you already have it? How?", "What if you can’t visualize vividly", "I wish people would do a demonstration when talking about things like this". Some push back on money talk: "how do i dress and take care of myself like i have money? with what money??"

## Communication style
Casual, friend to friend, a little skeptical, likes being let in on a method. Buyers praise it as a reset and a fast read: "a good read to reset mindset and commit to something and actually work it", "read through it fast read the whole book and you will learn a lot". Creators who sold it said it is "not just another book full of, like, mindful quotes and positive affirmations" and "more direct and actionable instead of just inspirational".

## What it means for the script
- Lead with the gap they feel (they read the books, still no steps), then show the steps in plain words. That is the selling point.
- Answer the comment questions inside the body: write it like you already have it, picture it, hold it, let go.
- No money or outcome promises (they already roll their eyes at "act like you have money"); talk about what you do, not what you get.
- Gift angle for the friend who makes a vision board every January; real timing = holidays and New Year goals.
- Keep it Grace-to-friends, no guru voice, no "secret society" talk.
