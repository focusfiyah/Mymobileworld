## Who
Haitians and Haitian Americans in the diaspora (New Jersey, New York, Miami, Boston), mostly women and parents, who want to show where they come from: at home, at a Flag Day parade, at a cultural event at school, on January 1. The seller who moved this same flag talks as a mom: "My son is in high school. He's always doing cultural events and always need a flag to represent." Search shows buying intent: "where to buy haitian flag", "where to buy haiti flag", "how to style a haitian flag", "how to celebrate haitian flag day".

## Pain point experience
Not a problem with a product: a pride gap. Showing up without a flag when everyone else has one ("not me forgot my flag at home"), the scramble before the day ("Flag Day May 18th is around the corner, don't be without your Haitian flag"), and the worry that the kids grow up far from Haiti and lose it ("I just want her to learn about her heritage so she could be proud of being Haitian", "we pass it on down to the kids"). They look closely at the flag itself: "dats not the Haitian flag. out palmis is not blue", "What size is this one ?". Some want the black and red flag ("Do they sell Black/Red flag?", "I rather the black and red"): this listing is the blue and red flag only.

## Communication style
Loud, proud, playful, mixed Kreyol and English, a lot of flag emojis: "HAPPY HAITIAN FLAG DAYY", "Yesssss!!!! Representing!!!", "li togo lal reprezante nou", "He’s an original!!! The flag say it all". The word that keeps coming back is represent ("don't be without your Haitian flag cause you want to represent!", "it's still important for us to represent the culture that we came from"). Talk like a cousin hyping you up, not a lecture on history. Grace already speaks this way: "One thing about Ayisyen? We represent where we come from!"

## What it means for the script
Lead with identity and the moment you don't want to be without it, not with fabric specs. Say "represent" and "where we come from" in Grace's voice. Show the flag big (wrap it on her, hold it open) and close in on the coat of arms and the palm tree, because this crowd checks the details. Answer "what size" by saying three by five and showing it on her body. Use one real date: January 1 (Independence Day, soup joumou) is the next big one; May 18 is the second. Make them want more than one: one to hang at home and one to carry. No black and red flag talk (not this product).
