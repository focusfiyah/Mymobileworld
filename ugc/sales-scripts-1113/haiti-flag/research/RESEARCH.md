## Product differentiation
A full-size 3x5 ft Haitian flag (blue and red with the coat of arms), printed (not embroidered) 150D polyester, dye-sublimated for bright colors, heavy canvas header with two solid brass grommets, made for inside or outside (TikTok title, G128 store). G128 is a flag maker. Competitors in the TikTok Shop "haiti flag" search: Flags Importer 3x5 dye-sublimated flag (833 sold, the category leader), Matou Boutique 3x5 with brass grommets, Gaia AYITI Suite imperial flag, flag jerseys and flag dresses (coach/shop-haiti-flag.json). What we can show that sells: it is big enough to wrap around her, the coat of arms and palm tree are sharp up close, and the brass grommets are there to hang it. No sold count or stars could be read for this G128 listing (captcha), so no number proof.

## Target buyer
Haitians and Haitian Americans in the diaspora, mostly women and parents, who want to show where they come from at home, at Flag Day parades (May 18), at January 1 Independence Day gatherings and at school cultural events. Search intent is real: "where to buy haitian flag", "where to buy haiti flag", "how to style a haitian flag", "how to celebrate haitian flag day" (audience/questions.md). The seller who moved this same flag speaks as a mom whose son "always need a flag to represent".

## Pain points and desires
Pain: being the one with no flag when everyone else pulls theirs out ("not me forgot my flag at home"), the last-minute scramble before the day ("don't be without your Haitian flag"), and the fear that kids raised away from Haiti lose the culture ("we pass it on down to the kids"). Desire: to represent, loud and proud ("Yesssss!!!! Representing!!!", "li togo lal reprezante nou"), to have a flag that looks right (they check the coat of arms: "out palmis is not blue"), big enough to wear, ready for every event.

## Buyer motivation
Why now: January 1 is Haitian Independence Day and soup joumou day (real date, 7 weeks out), holiday gatherings with family are coming (Thanksgiving Nov 26, Christmas), and Flag Day is May 18. A flag is the easiest way to show pride at all of them. Emotional reason: pride and belonging, being seen as Ayisyen, passing it to the kids.

- "Can I hang it?" -> two brass grommets, indoor/outdoor (title).
- "I want the black and red flag" -> not this product; we do not mention it.
- "Will it last outside?" -> we only say it is made for inside or outside (title); no fade or weather claim.

## Big Fish (why ladder)
- I don't have a Haitian flag at home.
- So when everyone pulls theirs out on Flag Day or January first, I'm the one with empty hands.
- That makes me feel like I'm not showing where I come from, living far from Haiti.
- And I worry my kids won't grow up proud of it.
Biggest feeling: wanting everyone, and my kids, to see I'm proud of where I come from ("we represent where we come from").

## Overall verdict
People buy this flag to represent: it is the simplest way to show Haitian pride at the next big moment (January 1, Flag Day, a family gathering). Lead with the moment of being the only one without a flag and the pride of representing; prove it with size on her body, a coat-of-arms close-up and the brass grommets; close on January first.
