## Who
Two buyers. (1) The snacker, mostly women buying for the house, who wants a savory snack that is not junk and has been let down by dry store sticks: "Great afternoon protein snack", "My family loves them for snacks." (2) The gift buyer, mostly women shopping for a husband, boyfriend or dad who is hard to buy for. Search shows it plainly: "beef jerky gifts for men", "beef jerky gift for dad", "beef jerky gift for boyfriend", "beef jerky christmas gift box". A smaller group needs it gluten free: "my boyfriend is gluten free (medically) and has an awful timr trying to find jerky/meat sticks".

## Pain point experience
The biggest one is being burned by bad jerky. They say it in the same words as Grace's own text: "It taste like cardboard with Taki powder", "i bought it and it tastes like cardboard, it doesn't even have jerky texture.", "it does not crunch like that it like card board dry asf no crunch at all I bought 2 never again..", "that's nasty worst 25 dollars I spent". Second is distrust of what is inside store sticks: "That list of ingredients though. Not good." and "the ingredients list is longer than my receipt from Walmart". For gifts, the pain is guessing ("is beef jerky a good gift") and wanting it to look like a real present; a buyer of this pack wrote "The packaging alone is a reason to but this."

## Communication style
Short, loud and casual: 'SOOO good', 'fire', '10/10'. They talk about taste in plain words ("you can actually taste the beef"), they share it ("I took mine to work and shared them with my co-workers"), and they buy again ("Had to order another"). They are quick to call out a bad buy, so a claim has to be shown, not hyped. Numbers like protein grams draw pushback ("Said 4g like it'll leave a dent"), so stay on taste, variety and who made it.

## What it means for the script
- Lead the voiceover with the cardboard pain in the buyer's own word ("cardboard"), the same angle as Grace's selling text, then answer it with real wood-smoked beef jerky and the review line "you can actually taste the beef".
- Blame the common enemy (dry gas station sticks), never the viewer.
- Talk-to-camera goes after the gift buyer: the guys who are hard to shop for, the box that shows up wrapped, Christmas as the real date.
- Proof is shown: each flavor held up, the jerky bag, "VETERAN OWNED" on the wrapper, the paper-wrapped box.
- No protein numbers, no "clean ingredients" claim, no sugar claim for the whole pack.
