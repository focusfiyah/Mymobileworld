# Audience: Shnuggle Toddler Bath Tub (2026-11-13)
Data: research/audience/questions.md (1,085 autocomplete phrases), comments.md (44 TikTok comments from Grace's video and 3 niche videos), voc.md (listing reviews, Hello Bee parent forum).

## Who
Moms (and grandparents) of a baby around 9 months to 3 years who has grown out of the infant tub. Buyers write "I love this tub I got it for my 1 year old", and two of the newest reviews are grandparents: "My grandson loves his new tub and cried when it was time to get out", "Very comfortable for my 10 month old grandson." A second group has no bathtub at all: "We only have showers at our house and this will fit her for a while." They search "toddler bathtub for stand up shower" and "toddler bathtub for walk in shower".

## Pain point experience
The in-between stage: "At 5.5 months, LO is starting to get too big for her infant tub." Then "Does that mean she goes straight into the big-girl tub (ie. bathtub)?" The big tub feels wrong: "he's too big for his little whale tub, but still seems scared to be in the big tub unless we're with him." So the parent props the child up by hand ("we would let him sit but always with our hands on him.") or climbs in: "I have to jump in the shower with her." The emotional part is bath time turning into crying and a fight at the end of the day: "First time her taking a bath without me having to join in." The tubs they tried before let them down: "The one I got from target is not working out." Doubts: "Does it actually fit in the bathtub? it looks bulky." and "where do you store it". Price is the loudest objection: "over priced" (top comment, 19 likes).

## Communication style
Warm, short, mom-to-mom talk, lots of my daughter, my 1 year old and boo (all in the comments). They describe the child's reaction, not specs: "She immediately jumped in", "cried when it was time to get out". They ask practical yes/no questions (does it fit, where do you store it). They use "big tub", "baby tub", "toddler tub", "LO".

## What it means for the script
- Hook on the stage everyone hits (baby tub too small, big tub too big), not on age numbers (no "for ages 1 to 3"); "you" language, broad (moms, grandmas).
- Pain beat = the private behavior: kneeling at the big tub holding the child up. Common enemy: tubs made only for babies or only for grown-ups.
- Proof shown, not said: her daughter sitting and leaning back in it, inside the big bathtub (answers "does it fit in the bathtub").
- Say "the big tub" and "the baby tub" the way buyers do. One trust line: 4.9 stars.
- Never answer the price comment with a number or deal talk; the value line is the brag ("she won't want to get out", from the grandparent review) and a second unit for grandma's house (grandparents are buyers).
