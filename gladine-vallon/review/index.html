Client review · Pennsylvania care home

Gladine Vallon: 10-bed care home plan

My read of the plan she sent, what has to change, what money is really out there, and the steps in order. Prepared for Ralph, 2026-11-10.

The short version

The idea is sound and the care values are good. The plan is about a quarter of a business plan: it describes care and staff, but has no license choice, no money, no location and no timeline. Three things decide whether this works:

Fix

The staffing plan loses money. A nurse and a CNA on every shift cost about $578,598 a year. At 10 residents the home would need about $7,700 per resident per month to break even. The Pennsylvania median is $6,480.

Decide

She has to pick a license. Pennsylvania splits these into Personal Care Homes and Assisted Living Residences. Her plan mixes the two. I recommend starting as a Personal Care Home.

Reset

There is no state grant to start a for-profit care home. The realistic money is an SBA bank loan plus a PIDA state loan plus about 20% of her own. Grants and training funds come after the home is licensed.

Good

Private rooms and baths, residents who walk and eat on their own, and a small size all fit the rules and are easy to sell to families.

License: which one, and why it matters

Both licenses come from the PA Department of Human Services. Her plan says "assisted living" and promises "nursing care" and "treatments." Only an Assisted Living Residence may offer extra health care services, and that license has much stricter building rules.

My recommendation: start as a Personal Care Home, replace the words "nursing care" with "medication help and health monitoring," and bring in an RN as a part-time consultant. If she wants Medicaid residents or more health care later, she can move up to an Assisted Living Residence, but only if the building was built to that standard.

The 20-bed goal needs checking before she buys anything. Under the model building code, homes with 6 to 16 residents are usually a residential class. Above 16 they usually become an institutional class, with much tougher construction and sprinkler rules. Pennsylvania adopted the 2021 codes on 2026-01-01. A local architect or code official needs to confirm the class for 20 residents at the actual property.

Staffing: the numbers

Pennsylvania's minimum is 1 hour of direct care per resident per day (2 hours for residents with mobility needs), with at least one staff member 21 or older on site at all times. For 10 residents who can walk, that is 10 hours a day. Her plan has 48.

The setup I put in the plan instead (10 residents)

  • One medication-trained aide around the clock, plus a second aide for 8 hours at the busiest time of day
  • An RN consultant for about 10 hours a week, plus on call
  • A cook and housekeeper for 20 hours a week
  • Gladine as the administrator, after the 100-hour PA course and test

Staff cost: about $361,400 a year ($3,012 per resident per month). Non-nurse staff may give oral, topical and drop medications after the DHS medication course, so the home keeps nurse oversight without paying for a nurse on every shift.

The grant

I checked state grant, loan and workforce programs, all as of today. Pennsylvania has no startup grant a for-profit care home can get. Keystone Communities and the Neighborhood Assistance Program are for towns and nonprofits. USDA Community Facilities is for public bodies and nonprofits. Civil money penalty grants are for certified nursing homes.

What every lender or grant will ask for

  • The full business plan (the completed draft below)
  • 3 to 5 years of financial projections, and a list of where the money comes from and goes
  • Proof she controls the property (purchase agreement or lease) and the zoning answer
  • Her licensing status and the administrator course certificate
  • Personal credit, tax returns, a personal financial statement and her 20%
  • Number of jobs created (PIDA expects about 1 job per $75,000 borrowed)
  • Letters of support, for example from hospital discharge planners or the county aging office
  • For federal programs: SAM.gov registration and a UEI number

Common reasons they say no: no property yet, no owner money, numbers that don't add up (like the current staffing), no care or management experience on paper, or a license that doesn't match the services.

Steps in order

  1. Months 1 to 2: form the LLC, get an EIN and a business bank account, start the 100-hour administrator course, meet her county economic development office and an SBA lender.
  2. Months 2 to 4: find the property; check zoning, the building class for 20 and the fire code; get contractor quotes.
  3. Months 4 to 6: close the loans and the property.
  4. Months 6 to 10: renovate, write the policies, get the fire safety approval.
  5. Months 9 to 11: apply to DHS, hire and train staff, start marketing to discharge planners and doctors.
  6. Months 11 to 12: DHS inspection, license, first residents.
  7. Months 13 to 24: fill to 10, apply for training funds and grants for licensed homes, prepare the 20-bed expansion.

DHS publishes no licensing timeline, so these months are an estimate.

What Gladine needs to get together

Open questions for her

  • Is she a nurse? If she is an RN or LPN, she can cover nurse oversight herself and the numbers get better.
  • Does she want Medicaid residents? That points to an Assisted Living Residence and a stricter building.
  • Does she already have a property in mind?
  • Will she run the home day to day, or hire an administrator?
