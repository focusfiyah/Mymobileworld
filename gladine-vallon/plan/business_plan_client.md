## 4. Resident Profile and Admission

### Who We Serve

Adults, typically 65 and older, who need help with daily activities or medications and who:

- Walk independently or with a walker or cane, and can leave the building on their own in an emergency within the time set by the Home's fire safety expert
- Eat independently or with assistance
- Do not need continuous skilled nursing care

### Admission Process

1. A tour and conversation with the resident and family
2. A medical evaluation completed by the resident's physician
3. An assessment by the Home's staff
4. A written support plan and signed admission agreement before move-in

### Transitions to a Higher Level of Care

When a resident's needs exceed what a personal care home may provide (for example, 24-hour skilled nursing, a feeding tube or IV therapy, or being unable to evacuate the building alone), the Home gives written notice, works with the family and physician, and helps arrange a move to an assisted living residence, a nursing facility or home care.

## 5. Market Analysis

### Industry Overview

Pennsylvania licenses two types of residential care for seniors: personal care homes and assisted living residences, both regulated by DHS. The statewide median cost of assisted living is $6,480 per month (CareScout Cost of Care Survey, 2025). Most residents pay privately; Medicaid does not pay personal care homes directly, although low-income residents may receive a state SSI supplement.

### Target Market

- Seniors 75 and older within about 10 miles of the Home who need daily assistance
- Adult children, often 45 to 65, who arrange care for a parent
- Hospital and rehabilitation discharge planners placing patients who cannot return home safely

## 6. Marketing and Referral Strategy

Most placements in senior care come through referrals. The Home will build relationships with:

- Hospital and rehabilitation discharge planners and social workers
- Physicians' offices, home health agencies and hospice teams
- The county Area Agency on Aging
- Churches, senior centers and community organizations
- Senior placement agencies, which typically charge a fee of about one month's rent

Supporting activities include a professional website, a Google Business Profile with photos and family reviews, printed brochures, and open houses with family tours before opening.

**Goal:** three residents signed before opening day, then one new resident each month until the Home is full.

## 9. Licensing and Regulatory Compliance

The Home will be licensed as a **Personal Care Home** under 55 Pa. Code Chapter 2600. This license permits every service the Home offers, including help with eating and mobility, for residents who can evacuate the building on their own.

| Requirement | Personal Care Home (Ch. 2600) |
|---|---|
| Licensing agency | PA Department of Human Services, Bureau of Human Services Licensing |
| Bedroom size | At least 80 square feet for a single room |
| Nursing | Not required; the Home retains a consultant RN |
| Direct care | At least 1 hour per resident per day |
| License fee | $15 |
| Inspection | DHS inspection before opening, then at least annually |

**Licensing steps:** form the LLC; secure the property; complete the administrator course; write policies; obtain fire safety approval; submit the DHS application (forms HS 633 and HS 2126, the affiliations form and the pre-licensure checklist) with the fee; pass the DHS inspection; admit residents.

Should the Home later choose to offer supplemental health care services or accept Medicaid residents, it may apply for an Assisted Living Residence license (55 Pa. Code Chapter 2800), provided the building meets that chapter's unit standards.

## 10. Facility

The Home will occupy a residential building with 10 private bedrooms, each with a private bathroom, plus a living room, dining room, kitchen and activity space. Before purchase or lease, the property will be confirmed for:

- Zoning approval for a personal care home
- Fire safety approval from the Department of Labor & Industry, the Department of Health or the local code official
- A building code classification that allows 20 residents. Homes with 6 to 16 residents are generally a residential occupancy (R-4); above 16 they are generally an institutional occupancy (I-1) with stricter construction and sprinkler requirements. A licensed architect will confirm the classification before purchase.
