### Business Concept

Glad Personal Care Home provides a residential housing option with personalized daily support, meals, and 24-hour safety supervision for seniors who can no longer live completely on their own.

The home will start with 10 residents, each with a private bedroom and a private bathroom. The small size lets staff know each resident well and help them stay as independent as they can.

Residents may walk on their own or with a walker or cane, as long as they can leave the building on their own in a fire within the time set by the home's fire safety expert. Residents may eat on their own or need help with eating. Staff help with ADLs based on each resident's written assessment and support plan.

The home provides personal care, medication management, a private bedroom and bathroom, meals and dining, housekeeping and laundry, caregivers on site around the clock with emergency call buttons, transportation, and social and recreational activities.

The goal is to grow from 10 to 20 residents within 24 months, after DHS approves the higher capacity.

**Funding needed:** about $1.04 million to buy and prepare the property and open, or about $297,000 if the building is leased (section 14), from the owner's own funds (about 20%), an SBA-backed bank loan and a PIDA state loan. After opening, the home will apply for workforce training funds and state grants for licensed homes.


## 2. Mission Statement

Our mission is to give residents a safe, clean, comfortable and welcoming home where they receive caring help while keeping their dignity, independence and quality of life.

We focus on care built around each person, medication safety, good meals, meaningful activities, time with others, and coordination of health services.


## 3. Vision

Glad Personal Care Home will be a trusted home known for kind and attentive care, a homelike setting, personal attention and engaging daily activities.

Residents should feel respected, valued and connected, not just looked after.


## 4. Initial Facility Capacity

The home will first serve 10 residents. Each resident will have:

- A private bedroom (at least 80 sq ft; we plan for larger)
- A private bathroom
- Use of the common living areas, dining room and recreation areas
- Scheduled activities
- Help with medications, given by staff who passed the DHS medication course
- Health monitoring and referral to outside nurses, home health and doctors when needed
- Help with ADLs as needed, including bathing, dressing, grooming, eating and mobility support
- Caregivers on site 24 hours a day, plus emergency call buttons or pull-cords in bedrooms and bathrooms
- Rides to medical appointments, grocery shopping and local outings
- Health care appointment coordination
- Meals and snacks

The home will be designed for resident safety, accessibility, comfort, privacy and independence.


## 5. Staffing Plan

Pennsylvania requires at least 1 hour of direct care per resident each day for residents who can walk, with at least one staff member 21 or older on site at all times. Our staffing goes well beyond that minimum. Medication-trained aides provide hands-on care around the clock, and a registered nurse oversees medications and resident health. This keeps a nurse involved in every resident's care while keeping the monthly rate affordable for families.

### Staffing model at 10 residents

| Role | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Direct care aide (med-trained) | 24 hours a day, every day | Always one staff member 21 or older on site, awake at night |
| Second direct care aide | 12 hours a day (7 a.m. to 7 p.m.), covering all three meals | Residents who need help eating get it without rushing; keeps the 75%-of-hours-while-awake rule met |
| Registered nurse (consultant) | About 10 hours a week, plus on call | Reviews medications and support plans, trains staff, checks residents after a change in condition |
| Cook / housekeeper | 20 hours a week | Aides cover the rest |
| Administrator | Gladine Vallon (owner) | Must finish the 100-hour PA administrator course and pass the test |

Aides will be CNAs where possible. Every aide who gives medications passes the DHS medication course and test, and the test is renewed every 2 years. Insulin needs extra diabetes training.

### Nursing responsibilities (the RN consultant)

Within the RN's legal scope of practice:

- Reviewing every resident's medications and medication records
- Training and checking the aides who give medications
- Checking residents after a fall, illness or other change in condition
- Advising on each resident's support plan
- Talking with doctors, pharmacies and hospitals when a resident's care needs it
- Advising when a resident's needs may be more than a personal care home can meet

### Aide responsibilities

Help with activities of daily living according to each resident's support plan:

- Bathing, dressing and grooming
- Toileting and personal hygiene
- Giving medications (trained aides), and recording each dose
- Taking vital signs when the support plan calls for it
- Telling the RN and the administrator about any change in a resident
- Serving meals and snacks, light housekeeping and laundry
- Running daily activities

### Staff training

- Before working alone: orientation, fire safety, resident rights and abuse reporting; at least one staff member trained in first aid and CPR on site at all times
- At least 12 hours of training a year for direct care staff (more if the home later becomes an Assisted Living Residence)
- PA State Police background check for every employee, plus an FBI fingerprint check for anyone who has not lived in Pennsylvania for the past 2 years

### At 20 residents

Two aides on site around the clock plus a third for 12 hours covering meals, an RN about 20 hours a week, a paid administrator, a full-time cook and a full-time housekeeper. The yearly staff cost rises to about $812,300, but the cost per resident drops to about $3,385 a month. At 10 residents the staff cost is about $400,000 a year ($3,333 per resident per month).


## 6. Licensing Path

Pennsylvania has two licenses for this kind of home. Both come from the Department of Human Services, Bureau of Human Services Licensing.

| | Personal Care Home (Ch. 2600) | Assisted Living Residence (Ch. 2800) |
|---|---|---|
| Health care | Help with ADLs and medications only | May offer "supplemental health care services" |
| Nurse | Not required | A licensed nurse in the building or on call at all times |
| Bedroom | 80 sq ft or more for one person | 225 sq ft in new buildings, not counting bath and closet (160 sq ft in buildings that existed before 2011) |
| Bathroom | A toilet and sink for every 6 residents | Toilet, sink and shower in every unit |
| Kitchen in each unit | Not needed | Kitchenette with a sink and outlets for a microwave and small fridge |
| License fee | $15 | $300 plus $75 per bed ($1,050 for 10 beds) |
| Medicaid | No | A few homes, through CHC plans |

**Why a Personal Care Home:** It fits a 10-bed home and a lower budget, and it allows every service the home offers, including help with eating, for residents who can get out of the building on their own. If Gladine later wants to offer more health care or take Medicaid residents, the home can apply to become an Assisted Living Residence, as long as the building was designed to that standard.

**Before buying or leasing a property**, the building needs:

- Zoning approval from the town for a personal care home
- A fire safety approval from L&I, the Department of Health or the local code official (renewed every 3 years)
- A building-code check for 20 residents. Under the model code, homes with 6 to 16 residents are usually a residential class (R-4), and homes with more than 16 are usually an institutional class (I-1), which has much stricter construction and sprinkler rules. A local architect or code official must confirm how Pennsylvania classifies this building before purchase.

**Licensing steps:** form the LLC, secure the property, finish the administrator course, write the policies, get the fire safety approval, send the application (forms HS 633, HS 2126, the affiliations form and the pre-licensure checklist) with the fee, pass the DHS inspection, then admit residents.


## 7. Services and Fees

**What Glad Personal Care Home provides:**

- **Personal care assistance:** help with bathing, dressing, grooming, eating and mobility support
- **Medication management:** organizing, tracking and giving daily prescriptions and doses, by staff who passed the DHS medication course
- **Housing:** a private bedroom and private bathroom for every resident

- **Housekeeping and laundry:** regular room cleaning, trash removal, linen changes and personal laundry
- **24/7 staff and safety systems:** caregivers on site around the clock, plus emergency call buttons or pull-cords in bedrooms and bathrooms
- **Transportation:** scheduled group or individual rides to medical appointments, grocery shopping and local outings
- **Social and recreational activities:** group fitness classes, game nights, hobby clubs, guest speakers and community events

## 8. Admission and Discharge

**Who we admit:** adults who need help with ADLs or medications, who walk on their own or with a walker or cane, and who can leave the building on their own in a fire in the time the fire safety expert sets. Residents may eat on their own or need help with eating.

**Before move-in:** a medical evaluation from the resident's doctor, an assessment by our staff, and a written support plan.

**When a resident needs more care than we can give:** for example 24-hour skilled nursing, a feeding tube or IV, or no longer being able to use a walker or cane to get out of the building alone. We give written notice, work with the family and the resident's doctor, and help them move to an assisted living residence, a nursing home or home care. The plan names nearby homes we can refer to.

### Ways to lower the startup cost

The $1,040,000 above assumes buying a $650,000 property. The property is 63% of the total, so the choice of building has the biggest effect on the startup cost. All figures are estimates until the city is chosen.

| Option | What changes | Startup total | Owner's 20% |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Buy at $650,000 (plan above) | Nothing | $1,040,000 | $208,000 |
| B. Buy at $650,000, trim the rest | A one-level house that already has an alarm or sprinklers (renovation $100,000), furniture $45,000, hiring $20,000, cash cushion $110,000 | about $940,000 | about $188,000 |
| C. Buy a cheaper house | A home around $500,000 in a lower-cost area, close to code already (renovation $100,000), cash cushion $120,000 | about $810,000 | about $162,000 |
| D. Lease the building | Rent a house that already meets the rules, about $6,000 a month plus a 2-month deposit; renovation about $75,000 (landlords often share it); cash cushion $120,000 | about $297,000 | about $59,000 |
| E. Lease with an option to buy | Same as D now; part of the rent can count toward buying the building later | about $297,000 | about $59,000 |

**Monthly costs barely change:** a $6,000 lease is close to the $6,500 monthly loan payment in the projections.

**Before choosing a lease:** the landlord must agree in writing to a personal care home, the lease should run 5 to 10 years (lenders and DHS want stability), and the owner builds no ownership in the building unless the lease includes an option to buy.

**What should not be cut:** the cash cushion. Year 1 runs about $101,300 short while the beds fill, so every option keeps at least $110,000.

**About grants:** Pennsylvania has no state grant to start a for-profit care home. These can help after the home opens:

- **PA Department of Health, long-term care Quality Investment Program:** up to $2,500 per licensed bed for personal care homes and assisted living. The 2026 round closed October 1, 2026, and the next round is not announced.
- **PA CareerLink on-the-job training:** pays about 50% of a new hire's wages for up to 6 months.
- **The local healthcare Industry Partnership:** free training for aides.
- **WEDnetPA:** up to $2,000 per employee a year for training. Past rules left out assisted living, so check this year's guide.
- **USDA Business & Industry loan guarantee:** instead of SBA, if the property is in a rural area.

## 17. Risks and How We Handle Them

| Risk | What we do |
|---|---|
| Residents need more care over time (eating, moving around) | Care fees set by assessment; reassess often; a second aide at every meal; transfer when a resident can no longer get out of the building alone |
| Beds fill slowly | Start marketing 3 months before opening; build relationships with discharge planners; 6-month cash cushion |
| Hard to hire and keep aides | Pay at or above the local rate; use CareerLink on-the-job training; build a list of fill-in staff |
| Inspection problems | Write all policies before applying; do mock inspections; keep training records up to date |
| A resident needs more care than we can give | Clear admission rules; written transfer process; list of nearby homes we can refer to |
| Building can't be approved for 20 | Confirm the building class and zoning for 20 before buying |
| Insurance costs | Get quotes for liability and property insurance before setting rates |
