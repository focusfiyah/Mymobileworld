# Fungix: 5 new script angles for Grace

**Ready to film as written.** Nothing here asks Grace for her own story or experience. Every line is about the viewer or the product label, so there are no blanks to fill and no personal claims to check.

Filmed like the first shoot: **hook, body and CTA as separate files, 3–4 takes of each**, so every angle can be cut into several combo ads.

## Product facts used (from the brand's listing)

Approved Science Fungix is a brush-on nail serum with **25% undecylenic acid**, plus tea tree oil and vitamin E. Brand directions:

- Clean and dry the nail.
- Brush a thin layer onto the affected nail **2–3 times a day**.
- Let it dry and absorb before covering the nail.
- Once a week, lightly roughen the nail surface with an emery board.
- Keep the brush off healthy nails.

Every "how to use it" line below comes from these directions. The brand's "results within a month" line is **not used**, because timeframes aren't allowed.

## The psychology every script uses

| Principle | What it means for Fungix |
|---|---|
| **Pain first** | The hook names something the viewer already hates: tucking toes under the table, dark polish every time, socks at the beach. |
| **Sell the moment** | Put them in the exact moment they feel it: shoes off at a friend's house, the salon polish wall, the beach. |
| **Move away from pain** | Getting away from embarrassment works faster than promising pretty nails. The product is the way out. |
| **Clarity, not convincing** | Explain calmly why what they're doing now (hiding it, painting over it) doesn't fill the gap. No pressure words. |
| **Remove uncertainty** | Every body answers what's in it, how to use it, and how often. |
| **Detachment builds trust** | One honest caveat in every body (2–3 times a day, every day, so skip it if you won't keep up). The CTA gives permission to scroll on. |

**Words to avoid:** "you NEED this", "obsessed", "game changer", "miracle".

## Compliance and edit rules from the first Fungix job

- **Claim wording.** Every body says this line word for word: *"It's made to target fungal growth and support healthier-looking nails."* Never say cures, kills, gets rid of, or fixes, and never give a timeframe.
- **No personal-use lines.** Grace never says "I use it" or "it worked for me", so nothing needs to be true for her personally.
- **Keep "This oil has…" in the body.** The approved dark search-result card is timed to "has" after "oil".
- **Hook overlays.** The condition photo collage comes in on "yellow" or "thick" and leaves on "funky".
- **Photos and urgency.** Condition-only photos are fine, but no before/after. No "selling out"; use *"don't put it off."*
- **Say numbers as words:** "twenty-five percent", "two to three times a day".
- **Length.** About 30 words for the hook, 60 for the body and 20 for the CTA, which comes to about 35s once the pauses are cut.
- **Different on-screen text per video.** Each angle has 3 bubble options.

## 1. Boots season (timing)

**The moment:** a whole summer of toes tucked under the table, and relief now that boots are back.
**The psychology:** Pain first, then a low-pressure reason to start now. Nobody's looking, so there's no risk. This is urgency without false scarcity.

**Bubbles**
- "sandals are going away… so is your excuse"
- "boots season = the secret toenail season"
- "start while nobody's looking at your feet"

**Hook takes**
- A: "If you spent all summer tucking your toes under the table so nobody saw the yellow, thick, funky nail, I see you. And now that boots are back, you're about to forget about it until next June."
- B: "Nobody is looking at your feet for the next six months. That's exactly why now is the time to deal with your toenails, not the week before sandal season."

**Body**
- *Clarity:* "Hiding it in boots doesn't do anything for it. It just buys you time."
- *Claim:* "This is Fungix. This oil has twenty-five percent undecylenic acid, and it's made to target fungal growth and support healthier-looking nails."
- *Remove uncertainty:* "You brush a thin layer on the nail two to three times a day, and let it dry before your socks go on."
- *Honest caveat:* "It's not one-and-done. You have to keep up with it."

**CTA (detached)**
- "If your toes don't bother you, skip this. If they do, it's in the orange cart. Start while they're in boots anyway."
- "Link's right here. Don't put it off till sandal season."

**Shots:** boots going on, the brush on a nail, the bottle label.

## 2. Stop painting over it (cover-up)

**The moment:** standing at the salon wall and always reaching for the darkest red.
**The psychology:** Sell the moment (a specific, recognisable habit), then clarity. Polish covers it and doesn't do anything for it. The viewer gets there on their own.

**Bubbles**
- "if your toe polish is always dark red… I know why"
- "painting over it isn't a plan"
- "the polish is hiding it, not helping it"

**Hook takes**
- A: "If you always pick the darkest polish at the salon, I know exactly why. You're covering up the yellow, thick, funky nail instead of doing anything about it."
- B: "Painting over a yellow toenail is hiding it, not helping it. Let's talk about it."

**Body**
- *Clarity:* "Polish makes it look fine for a week. Then it comes off, and it's the same nail underneath."
- *Claim:* "This is Fungix. This oil has twenty-five percent undecylenic acid, and it's made to target fungal growth and support healthier-looking nails."
- *Remove uncertainty:* "It's a little brush. Thin layer on the nail, two to three times a day, and let it dry and soak in."
- *Honest caveat:* "It only works for you if you stay consistent, so if you won't keep up with it, don't bother."

**CTA (detached)**
- "Not for everyone. But if you're done hiding it, it's in the orange cart."
- "It's linked right here. Don't put it off."

**Shots:** a remover pad wiping dark polish off a nail (use a hand or a stand-in foot model if you like, since it doesn't have to be Grace's), then the brush and the label.

## 3. Read the label first (ingredient)

**The moment:** staring at 20 toenail products online with no idea which one is real.
**The psychology:** Remove uncertainty is the whole ad. Grace hands the viewer a simple rule for choosing (clarity, not convincing). The approved search-result card shows the claim instead of Grace pushing it.

**Bubbles**
- "read the label before you buy a toenail oil"
- "the one ingredient to look for"
- "not all toenail oils are the same"

**Hook takes**
- A: "Before you buy any toenail oil, flip it over and read the ingredients, because a lot of them are basically just scented oil. There's one ingredient you want to see."
- B: "There are like fifty toenail oils and they all look the same. Here's how to tell them apart in five seconds."

**Body**
- *Clarity:* "Look for undecylenic acid." [Card appears.]
- *Claim:* "This oil has twenty-five percent. It's Fungix, and it's made to target fungal growth and support healthier-looking nails. It's also got tea tree oil and vitamin E."
- *Remove uncertainty:* "You brush it on the nail two to three times a day."
- *Honest caveat:* "The only catch is that you have to be consistent. Every day, no skipping."

**CTA (detached)**
- "Don't take my word for it, read the label. It's in the orange cart."
- "Tap the link and check the ingredients before you buy anything else."

**Shots:** scrolling a phone (hook), then the bottle turned so the ingredient panel faces the camera, then the brush.

## 4. You're probably doing it wrong (how to use it)

**The moment:** someone who bought a toenail product, used it twice and gave up.
**The psychology:** Remove uncertainty by teaching. It's a tutorial, so it looks like normal content rather than an ad, and it answers "how do I use it" before anyone asks. It works on people who've already tried something and gave up.

**Bubbles**
- "if you tried a toenail oil and gave up… watch this"
- "how to actually use toenail oil"
- "4 steps nobody tells you"

**Hook takes**
- A: "If you bought a toenail oil, used it twice and gave up, it's probably how you were using it. Here's how you're supposed to do it."
- B: "Nobody tells you how to actually use toenail oil, so here it is in four steps."

**Body**
- *Remove uncertainty, step by step:* "Step one, clean and dry the nail. Step two, once a week, lightly buff the top with a nail file."
- *Claim:* "Step three, Fungix. This oil has twenty-five percent undecylenic acid, and it's made to target fungal growth and support healthier-looking nails. Thin layer, only on the nail you're treating."
- *Detail:* "Step four, let it dry before socks."
- *Honest caveat:* "And do it two to three times a day. That's the part people skip."

**CTA (detached)**
- "If you're going to do it, do it right. It's in the orange cart."
- "Linked right here. Save this so you remember the steps."

**Shots:** one clip per step (towel, nail file, brush, socks). Say the step numbers so the cuts land cleanly. Hands or feet can be anyone's.

## 5. Buy it for your dad (gift)

**The moment:** the relative who won't take their socks off at the beach.
**The psychology:** It takes the shame out of buying. Nobody has to admit it's their own foot, so the pain is felt on someone else's behalf. The "tag someone" hook drives shares, and it reaches buyers shopping for parents and partners. Grace never says it's her dad; it's all "your".

**Bubbles**
- "your dad will never buy this for himself"
- "buying this for your dad is a love language"
- "for the person in your life who hides their feet"

**Hook takes**
- A: "Your dad is never going to buy something for his toenails, so you're going to have to do it for him. Every family has someone with the yellow, thick, funky toenails, and you know exactly who it is."
- B: "Tag the person in your family who will not take their socks off at the beach."

**Body**
- *Clarity:* "He's never going to walk into a store and ask for this. So don't make him."
- *Claim:* "It's Fungix. This oil has twenty-five percent undecylenic acid, and it's made to target fungal growth and support healthier-looking nails."
- *Remove uncertainty:* "It's a little brush, so it's easy. A thin layer two to three times a day, let it dry, done."
- *Honest caveat:* "The hard part is getting him to remember. So yes, you'll be reminding him."

**CTA (detached)**
- "Get one for your dad. It's in the orange cart."
- "Send this to whoever needs it. Link's right here."

**Shots:** a bottle handed across (hands only works), then the brush and the label.

## Filming notes for Grace

- **Say it as written.** Every line is ready; there's nothing to personalise.
- **Takes.** Film each hook, body and CTA 3–4 times, with a clear pause between takes. Keep every body take complete from start to finish, because we keep body takes paired and don't mix pieces.
- **Camera.** Use the camera's original HD files (1080p .MOV), not exported copies.
- **Say the claim line exactly as written** in every body take, with numbers as words: "twenty-five percent", "two to three times a day".
- **Honest caveat.** Say it calmly, like a friend, not like a disclaimer.
- **Pace.** Normal energetic pace. We cut every pause in the edit.
- **Close-ups.** Bottle label, brush, socks and boots. Hands and feet in close-ups don't have to be Grace's.
